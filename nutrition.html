Recovery + Heart

Magnesium Glycinate

1st Phorm
Dose200-400 mg
TimingEvening (helps sleep)
WithA small meal — empty stomach can upset
Why. Muscle relaxation, sleep quality, electrolyte balance. Mild evidence for reducing arrhythmia risk, especially when low magnesium is the trigger. Glycinate is the gentle, well-absorbed form. Heads up: recurrent arrhythmia after alcohol is "holiday heart" — magnesium helps but a cardiologist should confirm it's benign.

Heart Health

Gain weight, keep the heart happy.

Most "bulks" go wrong by piling on sodium, processed fats, and alcohol. None of that is required to grow.

What to actually do

Four habits that matter.

Drop the after-drinking arrhythmia trigger.Holiday heart

Arrhythmia in the day after drinking is well-documented — called holiday heart syndrome. Alcohol disturbs electrolytes (especially magnesium and potassium), dehydrates you, and irritates the heart's electrical system.

What helps: cap drinks at 1-2/night, hydrate aggressively (a glass of water between drinks and before bed), take magnesium that evening, eat a meal with potassium (banana, potato, salmon, leafy greens) before drinking.

What you should also do: if it's recurring, get a one-time cardiologist check. Most likely benign, but worth confirming once. Don't trust me on this part.

Aim for the lower end of the sodium range.1,500-2,000 mg

FDA cap is 2,300 mg. American Heart Association recommends 1,500 mg for anyone with heart concerns. Your meals stack fast: Glaze teriyaki + Chick-fil-A in the same day = 3,600 mg before snacks.

Quick wins: half-sauce on teriyaki (-700 mg), grilled instead of fried at fast food (-400 mg), home cook 4-5 dinners/week.

Get omega-3s in 3-5×/week.From food, not pills

Salmon (lunch + a couple dinners covers it). Walnuts, chia/flax, sardines also count. Omega-3s lower triglycerides, blood pressure, and arrhythmia risk.

If salmon-fatigued: a fish oil capsule (2-3 g EPA+DHA daily) is a fine substitute. Look for IFOS-certified brands.

Vegetables. Every day. No skipping.Boring but real

Leafy greens, peppers, broccoli, sweet potato. They give potassium and magnesium (counter sodium), fiber (cardiovascular and gut), and the micronutrients no supplement covers.

Easy add-ons: spinach in the smoothie (you won't taste it), a side of broccoli with home dinners, salad with fast-food choices.

Habits

The non-food multipliers.

Track for 2 weeks, then stop.Calibration, not lifestyle

Use MyFitnessPal or Cronometer for two weeks. Log everything honestly. You'll learn what 3,300 cal actually looks like on your plate. After that, you can eyeball it.

Weigh yourself 3-4 morning/week. If 2-week average isn't moving up, add 200 cal/day. If it's moving up faster than 1 lb/week, you're gaining fat — pull 200 cal back.

Sleep is part of the program.7-9 hours

Without sleep, weight gain skews toward fat. Recovery hormones (testosterone, growth hormone) drop. Hunger hormones (ghrelin) rise — but you're already trying to eat a lot, so the issue is recovery quality.

The magnesium will help you fall asleep faster.

Eat 4-5 times a day, not 3.Easier surplus

3,300 cal in three meals = 1,100 cal each. That's a lot of food. Splitting across 4-5 (3 meals + 2 snacks) makes hitting the surplus painless.

Easy snacks: Greek yogurt + honey + nuts (400 cal, 25 g P), protein shake + banana (350 cal, 30 g P), 2 boiled eggs + apple (300 cal, 13 g P), beef jerky + cheese stick + crackers (350 cal, 25 g P).

Protein, every meal.30-40 g per sitting

Muscle protein synthesis caps around 30-40 g per meal. Spreading 165 g across 4-5 meals is more effective than slamming 100 g at dinner.

Anchor each meal/snack to one protein source: eggs, chicken, salmon, beef, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey, jerky.

Pre and post lift.Carbs in, protein after

1-2 hours before: a meal with carbs and some protein (lunch is perfect timing if you lift in the late afternoon). Energy in the tank.

Within 1-2 hours after: protein shake or a meal with 30+ g protein. The "anabolic window" is wider than people think — don't stress the exact minute.

Ultra-processed food: fine, but not the bulk.80/20

Chick-fil-A and Panda Express aren't poison — they're calorie-dense and convenient, which helps when you're chasing a surplus. But they're also where the sodium, sugar, and seed oils stack up.

Rule of thumb: 80% of your meals from whole-ish food (salmon, beef, rice, eggs, oats, fruit, vegetables, whey). 20% from whatever you want.
